Onchain Research Note Aug 2026
The airdrop question

Give it away, watch it leave

Six DeFi projects, two ways to pay for growth. Three used their own token as the marketing budget — airdrops and reward payments. Three paid with cash or stablecoins and never handed the token out. The question this note tests: when you give your token away to acquire users, do the people who receive it actually stay?

00/

Quick word list

A few terms come up repeatedly. Here's each one in a single line so the rest reads smoothly.

AIRDROP — free tokens sent to wallets, usually to reward past activity or attract attention.
BOUNTY — a token or cash reward paid for completing a task: a post, a bug report, a referral.
LIQUIDITY MINING — paying users in your token to deposit funds in your app so it has capital to trade against.
HOLDER COUNT — how many wallets hold the token at a given moment; it rises whenever new buyers arrive.
COHORT — one specific group of wallets — say, everyone who received the airdrop — tracked as a unit over time.
DIAMOND HANDS — wallets that hold for the long run instead of selling quickly; the opposite of a flipper.
01/

The short answer

Yes — but read the small catch

Projects that funded growth with their own token gained an enormous holder base almost overnight — then watched nearly all of it sell and leave. Only a small fraction of the wallets that received Uniswap's and Jupiter's airdrops were still holding a year or two later. The cash-funded projects never got that overnight surge, but the smaller base they kept proved far stickier.

Here's the catch: total holder count and whether the original recipients stayed are two different measures. Arbitrum's holder count actually grew from about 340,000 to 2.4 million even as its airdrop recipients cashed out — new buyers simply replaced them. So the real question isn't the headline holder number; it's what happened to the specific wallets that received the tokens.

The airdrop cliff

This is the core evidence. We take the exact wallets that received each airdrop and track how many still hold as time passes. Both lines drop sharply in the first few weeks and keep falling to almost nothing. The dots are real, sourced figures; the line between them is simply connected.

Reach vs. staying power

The whole thesis on one map. Giving the token away buys a large crowd, but the crowd doesn't stick. Paying with cash reaches fewer wallets, and those wallets stay. The horizontal axis is real (wallets reached); the vertical axis shows the direction of staying power, not an exact percentage.

07/

The token-funded three, up close

UniswapUNI

The famous one. In Sept 2020 it sent 400 UNI to every past user — 150M coins across 251,534 wallets — plus a short-lived program that paid UNI for depositing funds.

About 55% sold within ~8 days. Around 93% eventually sold all of it. Only ~7% still held after ~2 years, and ~98% never participated in governance. One analyst framed it as a ~$351M spend that earned back only ~$208M in fees — a slow payback.

ArbitrumARB

The count trap. In March 2023 it distributed 1.16B ARB to 625,143 wallets, plus a 71M-ARB reward program for apps building on it.

Within a month, ~14% of the airdropped coins were sold on exchanges and ~30% moved into trading pools; large wallets sold heavily on day one. Yet total holders still grew from ~340k to ~2.38M — new buyers replaced those who left, which hides the churn if you watch only the headline number.

JupiterJUP

Solana's biggest, repeated yearly. "Jupuary" gave away 40% of all JUP across drops in 2024, 2025 and 2026 — worth ~$1.27B valued at each drop day's price. Round 1 alone reached ~955k wallets.

Of 639,109 round-1 claimers, only 60,697 (~9.5%) still held at the July-2024 cutoff. The 2025 drop pushed the price down ~9–10% in a day. The 2026 drop was cut ~71% to ease the selling pressure, and the community paused its own votes in mid-2025 over "a breakdown in trust."

The CAC math

Jupiter paid to reach ~2M users — and kept ~190k

CAC = the cost to acquire one user. Take the JUP given away, valued at each drop day's average price (R1 1B×~$0.63 + R2 700M×~$0.85 + R3 200M×$0.20 ≈ ~$1.27B), spread across the ~2M unique wallets reached. (Rounds overlap heavily, so ~2M is the widest reach, not the sum of every round.) Retention uses the measured ~9.5% still-holding rate.

Airdropping to an undefined crowd — with no check for whether a wallet actually holds long-term — means roughly 9 of every 10 dollars bought a user who was gone by the next quarter. Screening for proven holders before the drop is what turns that spend back into retention.

08/

The cash-funded three, up close

MakerDAOMKR

The cleanest example. Started in 2015 with 1M MKR created for governance. It sold coins privately to funds (a16z, Polychain, Paradigm) for cash and ran on fees earned in its own stablecoin. Never airdropped, never paid MKR for deposits.

The result is a small (~100k), steady, insider-heavy base. Surplus revenue is used to burn MKR rather than hand it out. (Note: a 2024 rebrand to "Sky" later added token rewards — so use the pre-2024 MKR era for a clean read.)

0xZRX

Sold it, didn't gift it. Raised ~$24M in Aug 2017 by selling half the supply, then ran on that cash.

The fair asterisk: in 2019 it added rewards for market-makers — but those are paid in ETH from fees, not free ZRX, so the "no token giveaway" label holds. Its base is mature and roughly flat at ~189k holders.

AaveAAVE*

Cash first, rewards later. ETHLend raised ~$16.2M selling LEND in a 2017 ICO. No airdrop — the 2020 LEND→AAVE move was a 100:1 swap for existing holders, not a giveaway to new users.

The honest asterisk: from late 2020–2021 Aave paid AAVE as staking (Safety Module) and deposit rewards. Its base is mature at ~187k holders, concentrated among long-term stakers.

Pull it live yourself — Dune queries

Two queries you can paste and run in Dune (set the engine to DuneSQL). The first counts holders month by month. The second is the important one — it rebuilds the "airdrop cliff" by tracking the exact wallets that received the drop.

Query 1

Holders, month by month

-- Holder count per month for one ERC-20 (Ethereum). Swap the contract for UNI/MKR/ZRX/AAVE. -- For ARB, change erc20_ethereum -> erc20_arbitrum. WITH flows AS ( SELECT "to" AS wallet, CAST(value AS double) AS amt, evt_block_time AS t FROM erc20_ethereum.evt_Transfer WHERE contract_address = 0x1f9840a85d5aF5bf1D1762F925BDADdC4201F984 -- UNI UNION ALL SELECT "from" AS wallet, -CAST(value AS double) AS amt, evt_block_time FROM erc20_ethereum.evt_Transfer WHERE contract_address = 0x1f9840a85d5aF5bf1D1762F925BDADdC4201F984 ), bal AS ( SELECT wallet, t, SUM(amt) OVER (PARTITION BY wallet ORDER BY t) AS running FROM flows ), months AS ( SELECT date_trunc('month', d) AS m FROM unnest(sequence(DATE '2020-09-01', current_date, INTERVAL '1' MONTH)) AS x(d) ), snap AS ( SELECT m.m, b.wallet, b.running, row_number() OVER (PARTITION BY m.m, b.wallet ORDER BY b.t DESC) AS rn FROM months m JOIN bal b ON b.t < m.m + INTERVAL '1' MONTH ) SELECT m AS month, count_if(running > 0) AS holders FROM snap WHERE rn = 1 GROUP BY m ORDER BY m;
Query 2

The airdrop cliff — cohort retention

-- What % of the airdrop wallets still hold, month by month. This draws the §04 curve. WITH recipients AS ( -- 1. freeze the airdrop crowd = wallets funded by the claim contract SELECT DISTINCT "to" AS wallet FROM erc20_ethereum.evt_Transfer WHERE contract_address = 0x1f9840a85d5aF5bf1D1762F925BDADdC4201F984 -- UNI AND "from" = 0x090D4613473dEE047c3f2706764f49E0821D256e -- UNI airdrop distributor ), flows AS ( -- 2. every transfer for just those wallets SELECT "to" AS wallet, CAST(value AS double) AS amt, evt_block_time AS t FROM erc20_ethereum.evt_Transfer WHERE contract_address = 0x1f9840a85d5aF5bf1D1762F925BDADdC4201F984 AND "to" IN (SELECT wallet FROM recipients) UNION ALL SELECT "from", -CAST(value AS double), evt_block_time FROM erc20_ethereum.evt_Transfer WHERE contract_address = 0x1f9840a85d5aF5bf1D1762F925BDADdC4201F984 AND "from" IN (SELECT wallet FROM recipients) ), bal AS ( SELECT wallet, t, SUM(amt) OVER (PARTITION BY wallet ORDER BY t) AS running FROM flows ), months AS ( SELECT date_trunc('month', d) AS m FROM unnest(sequence(DATE '2020-09-01', current_date, INTERVAL '1' MONTH)) AS x(d) ), snap AS ( SELECT m.m, b.wallet, b.running, row_number() OVER (PARTITION BY m.m, b.wallet ORDER BY b.t DESC) AS rn FROM months m JOIN bal b ON b.t < m.m + INTERVAL '1' MONTH ) SELECT s.m AS month, count_if(s.running > 0) AS still_holding, (SELECT count(*) FROM recipients) AS cohort_size, 100.0 * count_if(s.running > 0) / (SELECT count(*) FROM recipients) AS pct_still_holding FROM snap s WHERE s.rn = 1 GROUP BY s.m ORDER BY s.m;

Tip: these scan every transfer, so they can run a minute or two. To speed up, sample quarterly (change '1' MONTH → '3' MONTH) or add a date cap.

10/

So what should a project actually do?

The takeaway

Giving your token away to an open crowd buys reach, not loyalty. In every token-funded case here, more than 90% of recipients were gone within months. Free tokens attract fast flippers by design.

The fix isn't to stop airdropping. It's to target diamond hands — wallets with a track record of holding what they earn instead of dumping it the same day. If a project is going to distribute tokens, bounties, or rewards, it should send them to wallets that have already proven they hold.

In practice that means:

  • Score wallets before you distribute. Check whether an address held past airdrops and rewards for months rather than selling within days.
  • Reward holding, not just participation. Tie eligibility — or the size of the reward — to how long a wallet has held similar tokens.
  • Add a lock-up or claw-back. Vesting and "sell early, forfeit part of it" rules push a giveaway closer to the sticky cash-funded pattern.
  • Filter out serial flippers. The same wallets farm drop after drop; a history check screens them out before they cost you.

How to spot diamond hands: Nansen tags wallets with a "Diamond Hands" label, and you can build the same idea in Dune — flag any wallet that received past airdrops or rewards and still held them 3, 6, or 12 months later (Query 2 above is exactly that check, just pointed at whichever token you're screening). Uniswap's ~98% governance no-show is the warning sign: a large giveaway buys holders on paper, not committed supporters.
