The diamond-hands case study

The airdrop that held

Most airdrops bleed out — recipients sell on receipt and the price caves. One didn't. $ANSEM held a ~$100M+ market cap while airdropping tokens on repeat, by doing one thing differently: it screened wallets and rewarded the holders. Here's how it worked, and what it means for anyone distributing tokens.

00/

The problem with a normal airdrop

The typical airdrop recipient is more likely to sell quickly than hold. That selling creates immediate pressure that can cascade into larger price declines — the token drops the moment it's distributed. In most cases more than 90% of recipients are gone within months, so a giveaway meant to build a community mostly funds an exit.

"Diamond hands" — wallets that hold for the long run instead of flipping — are the coveted exception. The obvious question no one operationalised: what if you deliberately identify and reward the people most likely to hold?

01/

The case study — $ANSEM

$ANSEM is a meme token launched by the crypto KOL Ansem (@blknoiz06, ~1.3M followers on X). Rather than distributing tokens indiscriminately, Ansem used BullpenFi's wallet analysis to process, classify and score airdrop claims based on each wallet's holding duration and engagement — letting distributions favour the users most likely to retain.

The result was a staggered, recurring airdrop — an "evergreen" drop designed to put more tokens into the hands of diamond hands while reducing immediate sell pressure from recipients, staggering each round at higher market caps and prioritising SOL rewards funded from other revenue.

STEP 01

Score every wallet

Process and classify airdrop claims by on-chain holding duration and X engagement — a behavioural profile per wallet, not a flat list.

STEP 02

Weight toward holders

Favour wallets more likely to retain; exclude those who sold immediately from the next distributions, so serial flippers don't get fed twice.

STEP 03

Stagger, don't dump

Release recurring drops over time at higher market caps, letting people de-risk gradually instead of all at once — cutting the sell-pressure spike.

02/

The result

Combining wallet-holding behaviour with X activity revealed which users were likely to hold or redistribute — and excluded immediate sellers from later rounds. That created a healthier price floor and let the token keep scaling even as tokens were still being handed out. After an all-time high near $0.45, $ANSEM was still trading around $0.28 at a ~$117M market cap weeks later — roughly $100M of market cap effectively protected by diamond-hand holders.

04/

What this means: the Holder Profile

The lesson generalises well beyond one meme token. Any project distributing tokens could benefit from analysing a "Holder Profile" the same way Web2 companies analyse their ICP (Ideal Customer Profile).

A holder's intentions are increasingly transparent through their past wallet behaviour. In Web2, cookies record user behaviour to help companies identify and target their ICP. In Web3, wallet history reveals who holds, who sells, who engages, and who creates long-term value — no tracking pixel required. It's already on-chain.

The opportunity

Use behavioural wallet data to distribute toward the people most likely to stay — raising LTH (Long-Term Holders) and, ultimately, user LTV (Lifetime Value).

In practice, that's the $ANSEM pattern, made repeatable:

  • Screen before you distribute. Score wallets on how they held past airdrops and rewards, not just whether they showed up.
  • Reward holding, exclude flippers. Weight the drop toward proven holders and drop immediate sellers from the next round.
  • Make it evergreen. Stagger recurring drops at higher caps so recipients de-risk gradually instead of dumping on day one.
